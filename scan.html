Question 1 of 8
Does your organisation use AI systems that make decisions affecting people?
This includes automated hiring, credit scoring, content moderation, access control, fraud detection, or any system where AI output influences a human outcome.
Question 2 of 8
Do you have a tamper-evident audit trail for every AI decision?
EU AI Act Article 12 requires high-risk AI systems to automatically log events in a way that cannot be altered. Logs must be retained and available to regulators.
Question 3 of 8
Can you explain every AI decision in plain language to a regulator?
Article 13 requires AI systems to be transparent. Every decision must include a human-readable explanation of which factors drove the outcome.
Question 4 of 8
Is there a human oversight pathway for high-risk AI decisions?
Article 14 requires that humans can override, correct, or halt AI systems. High-risk decisions must not be fully automated without human intervention.
Question 5 of 8
Have you conducted a formal risk assessment for your AI systems?
Article 9 requires a continuous risk management system covering identification, analysis, and mitigation of risks. This must be documented and updated throughout the AI lifecycle.
Question 6 of 8
Does your AI system interact with or make decisions affecting children?
AI systems affecting minors are automatically classified as high-risk and are subject to additional obligations under the UK Online Safety Act 2023 and ICO Children's Code.
Question 7 of 8
Are your AI systems registered with the EU AI Act database?
High-risk AI systems must be registered in the EU database before being placed on the market. This is a hard legal requirement from August 2026.
Question 8 of 8
When is your organisation planning to be fully EU AI Act compliant?
Enforcement begins August 2026. Fines reach 3% of global annual turnover or 15 million euros, whichever is higher.
