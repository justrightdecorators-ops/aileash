Question 1 of 8
Does your organisation use AI to make decisions that affect people?
This includes hiring tools, credit scoring, content moderation, fraud detection, access control, or any AI where the output influences what happens to a person.
Question 2 of 8
Do you have a tamper-proof audit trail for every AI decision?
EU AI Act Article 12 requires AI systems to log events automatically in a way that cannot be altered. Standard database logs your team can edit do not satisfy this. You need a cryptographic chain.
Question 3 of 8
Can you explain every AI decision in plain language to a regulator?
Article 13 requires transparency. Every decision must come with a human-readable explanation of exactly which factors drove the outcome. The algorithm decided is not an explanation.
Question 4 of 8
Can a human step in and override any AI decision?
Article 14 requires a human oversight pathway. High-risk decisions cannot be fully automated. You need a CHALLENGE state that routes to a real person before the decision is finalised.
Question 5 of 8
Have you done a formal, documented risk assessment for your AI?
Article 9 requires continuous risk management - not a one-time exercise. It must be documented, updated regularly and operational throughout the entire lifecycle of your AI system.
Question 6 of 8
Do children under 18 use or interact with your platform?
AI systems that affect children are automatically high-risk under the EU AI Act. The UK Online Safety Act 2023 and ICO Children's Code also apply - and both are already in force right now.
Question 7 of 8
Is your high-risk AI registered in the EU AI Act database?
High-risk AI systems must be registered in the official EU database before being placed on the market. This is a hard legal requirement from August 2026 - not a guideline.
Question 8 of 8
When will your organisation be fully EU AI Act compliant?
Enforcement begins August 2026. Fines reach 3% of global annual turnover or 15 million euros - whichever is higher. Per violation.
