Cryptographic Instruction Governance

AILeash Brain

A cryptographic instruction governance layer for AI systems. Every instruction hashed. Every decision sealed. Every action recorded in a tamper-evident SHA-256 chain — where altering one record breaks every record after it.

What It Is

A deterministic gate that records what your AI was told to do.

AILeash Brain sits between your AI system and the instructions it receives. Every instruction is converted into a SHA-256 hash and checked against a set of explicitly defined rules. Permitted instructions pass through in milliseconds. Recognised dangerous instructions are blocked instantly, and every decision — allowed or blocked — is sealed into an audit chain that is tamper-evident: any later edit breaks the chain and is detectable by anyone.

It is more than a keyword filter — word-level hashes, phrase-level hashes and pattern rules are checked independently, and inputs are normalised so trivial obfuscation is caught. It is honest about its boundary too: it stops known dangerous instructions and their close variants, not every possible paraphrase. What it guarantees is that every decision it makes is permanent, explainable and provable.

Benefits

What it does for your organisation.

Catches known prompt-injection patterns

Common instructions designed to override your AI's behaviour — "ignore previous instructions", "you are now" — are caught by hash and pattern rules, and every attempt is sealed as evidence.

Flags data-exfiltration attempts

Instructions that try to export, dump or extract user data are identified and blocked, and every attempt is recorded permanently in the chain.

Child-safety instruction blocks

Pre-built rules block known grooming and secrecy phrasings, helping prevent your AI from being turned against child users — recorded as evidence when they occur.

EU AI Act evidence trail

Every governance decision is sealed in a tamper-evident chain that anyone can verify — built for the record-keeping obligations of Article 12.

Detects compliance-bypass attempts

Instructions that try to disable logging, clear audit trails or override compliance are blocked and sealed as evidence of the attempt.

Verifiable audit trail

The SHA-256 chain produces tamper-evident evidence of every decision. Whether any specific record is admissible is a matter for a court — what the maths guarantees is that it hasn't been altered since it was sealed.

An honest word on scope. Brain stops recognised dangerous instructions and their close variants using cryptographic hash-matching and pattern rules. It is not a semantic AI and will not catch every possible paraphrase of a harmful instruction. What it does guarantee is that every decision it makes — and every change to its own rulebook — is deterministic, explainable, and permanently sealed where tampering is detectable by anyone.
The Standard

Declare it. Enforce it. Two files.

robots.txt governed the crawler era with one text file. The AI era needs two — and both live at this domain's root, first of their kind:

/ai.txt — the DECLARATION. How this domain's AI is governed: engine, decision model, audit construction, and the public endpoint where anyone can verify it. AI-TXT/1.0. /comply.txt — the ENFORCEMENT. The orders a machine must follow when it interacts with this domain: pre-flight reads, handshake, the gateway every instruction passes through, and what happens on violation — a BLOCK, sealed as evidence. COMPLY-TXT/1.0.

The discipline that makes them a standard rather than decoration: a declaration without a verify endpoint is marketing; an enforcement file without a live gateway is a wish. Every line in both files is backed by a route you can call right now. When AI agents begin choosing which platforms to transact with, these are the files they will read first — and Brain is the gateway standing behind them.
