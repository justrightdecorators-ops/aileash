The Sovereign Evidence Standard: tamper-evident governance for AI decisions, instructions, payments and identity

Justin Antony Dobson · Founder, Monop Content · sebbi.pro · July 2026 · Version 4.0 · Engine v6.5.0, live in production · Contents: architecture (§2) · eight instruments (§3) · the open standard (§4) · the capture doctrine (§5) · competitive landscape (§6) · sovereign deployments (§7) · economics (§8) · traction (§9) · investment case (§10)

Every era of computing ends with a reckoning about records. The web's first reckoning was security; its second was privacy; the one now arriving is evidence. The EU AI Act demands records of consequential AI decisions that survive hostile scrutiny. The UK's Online Safety Act demands provable duty of care. The 2024 mandatory reimbursement rules make banks and payers answerable for the care taken over every payment. Generative models have made identity itself contestable. In each case the required artefact is identical — a record that cannot have been quietly rewritten by anyone, including its keeper — and in each case the incumbent answer is a log that its keeper can edit. This paper describes a complete, live production system that generates true evidence as a by-product of normal operation; details its eight instruments; sets out a strategic doctrine — technical capture becoming forced capture — for how such a system wins its market; positions it against every competing category; and presents the case for funding its distribution.

1. The problem: the evidence gap at the heart of the digital economy

Ask any compliance vendor how their audit trail works and the answer is a variation of "we log everything." The unasked question decides legal outcomes: who can edit those logs? The answer is always somebody — a database administrator, an engineer with production access, the vendor itself. A record that can be silently altered has no evidentiary weight the moment its integrity is questioned, and integrity is precisely what regulators, courts, insurers and due-diligence teams now question first. This is the evidence gap: the digital economy runs on records, and almost none of its records are evidence.

Three legal regimes have converged on the gap simultaneously. The EU AI Act (Regulation 2024/1689) reaches general application in August 2026, with penalties up to 3% of global annual turnover or €15m per violation; its record-keeping duty (Article 12) presumes records a supervisory authority can rely on. (The proposed Digital Omnibus deferral moves the high-risk Annex III deadline to December 2027, pending formal adoption — a delay in enforcement, not in the obligation, and general-purpose duties proceed regardless.) The UK Online Safety Act 2023 is in force now; Ofcom's investigations turn on whether a platform can evidence its safety decisions rather than merely describe them, and the ICO has already fined a major platform £12.7m under the Children's Code. The Payment Systems Regulator's mandatory reimbursement rules (October 2024) oblige banks to reimburse authorised push payment fraud up to £85,000 unless the payer failed to take reasonable care — placing a legal premium on provable diligence at both ends of every payment.

A fourth pressure is not legal but epistemic: generative AI has collapsed the cost of fabricating profiles, voices, documents and quotes to near zero. When anything can be faked cheaply, the scarce commodity is no longer content — it is provenance. Markets reprice around scarcity; the repricing has begun.

The market's response to all four pressures has been dashboards, policy documents and AI-watching-AI: artefacts that demonstrate effort, not truth. What follows is the alternative: evidence by construction.

2. The architecture

2.1 A deterministic engine below the model layer

The engine scores every event across nine weighted signals and returns one of three verdicts — ALLOW, CHALLENGE or BLOCK — with plain-language reasons. Because scoring is fixed mathematics rather than a learned model, it is deterministic: identical inputs produce identical outputs, forever. Any regulator or engineer can recompute any historic decision from the public specification at /api/spec. This is a structural point, not a preference: probabilistic systems auditing probabilistic systems inherit the opacity they are meant to police. A governance layer that cannot explain itself arithmetically is a second defendant, not a witness. Deterministic systems are the only ones that can honestly satisfy Article 13.

Thresholds: ALLOW below 0.35; CHALLENGE from 0.35; BLOCK from 0.70. A CHALLENGE verdict carries a hosted human-verification URL — the Article 14 oversight pathway — whose resolution is itself sealed. The system fails safe: an unparseable event is BLOCKED and sealed, never waved through.

2.2 The hash chain: tamper-evidence by construction

Every decision is sealed at the moment it is made: hashed with SHA-256 together with the seal of the record before it, so each block's integrity depends on the entire history beneath it. Alter one character of one historic record and every subsequent seal fails verification — publicly, at an open endpoint, no account required. The operator cannot rewrite its own records.

A system that does not trust its own creator is the only kind whose records qualify as evidence.

seal(n) = SHA-256( seal(n−1) · timestamp · event · result )
verify: recompute from genesis; any mismatch localises tampering to a block index
latency: score + decide + seal + respond < 30ms inline; SonicBoom median 28ms
concurrency: tip read, hash and insert inside a single lock hold — no race window
resilience: automatic daily database backups; the chain survives restarts intact

2.3 Gapless receipts: proving what is absent

Tamper-evidence proves records were not altered; it does not prove records were not omitted — and selective disclosure is how sophisticated actors lie with true documents. Every sealed decision therefore receives a per-key, strictly monotonic sequence number issued in the same transaction as the seal. A client holding receipts 47 and 49 can prove arithmetically that 48 exists and is missing from any disclosure. Completeness stops being a claim and becomes a calculation — a property the author has not observed in any competing product. Reconciliation is self-service at /api/coverage.

2.4 Zero dependencies as security posture and pricing power

The entire platform is pure Python with no external packages: no frameworks to patch, no supply chain to audit, no transitive compromise surface — in an era in which supply-chain attacks are the dominant intrusion vector, the attack surface is one file its author wrote. It deploys as a single process and runs full production for approximately £10 per month. That figure is the pricing mechanism, not trivia. Incumbents charge £50,000 a year partly because their cost base demands it; a platform whose infrastructure costs less than a streaming subscription can charge 50p per device and remain profitable — and can give away 90 days at negligible marginal cost. Cost base is pricing power; architecture is the moat.

3. The eight instruments

One engine, one chain, eight ways in. Each instrument is a different constituency's door into the same evidence infrastructure — and each constituency, once inside, strengthens the chain for every other.

3.1 AILeash — AI decision governance

Who it is for: any organisation whose AI makes decisions about people — banks approving loans, insurers pricing policies, fintechs blocking payments, marketplaces banning accounts, HR platforms screening candidates.

What it does: one POST /api/govern call per decision returns the verdict, plain-English reasons, the seal, the chain block index and the gapless receipt sequence — the complete Article 9/12/13/14 evidence set, generated inline in under 30ms. BLOCK verdicts trigger a real-time alert email with the sealed evidence attached, throttled to one per hour per key so an attack cannot also flood the inbox. Live monitoring at /api/pulse; billing transparency at /api/usage.

Integration: any system that can send an HTTPS POST with JSON — every modern backend, no-code tools, mobile via the customer's own server. No SDK, no library, nothing added to the stack. A step-by-step installation guide with the customer's own key baked into the code samples is emailed automatically on signup.

3.2 Brain — cryptographic instruction governance

Who it is for: every organisation running AI on instructions — which is every organisation running AI. System prompts are now operating policy: they decide what an assistant will refuse, disclose and do. Yet almost no company can prove which instructions its AI was actually running last Tuesday.

What it does: Brain fingerprints and seals the instruction set itself. Each version of a system prompt or operating policy is hashed and locked to the chain with a timestamp, so an organisation holds provable answers to the questions that follow every AI incident: what were the instructions at the time? when did they change? who changed them? was the deployed prompt the approved prompt? When a prompt-injection incident, a rogue output or a regulator's Article 12 request arrives, the instruction history is evidence, not archaeology. Available at /brain. No competing category currently treats instructions as governed, versioned, sealable artefacts; Brain is, to the author's knowledge, the first instruction-governance layer in production.

3.3 SonicBoom — the compliance layer for existing AI stacks

Who it is for: companies already running AI on AWS, Azure, Google Cloud, OpenAI or Anthropic that need compliance without touching latency budgets.

What it does: one line of code alongside the existing AI call adds a sealed compliance record to every request — before the call to gate it, or after to seal it — at a median 28ms. Governance that never becomes the latency problem it exists to prevent. Powered by the same Sebdog engine core as AILeash: one engine, so one body of chain history, whichever door a customer enters through.

3.4 Sentinel — fraud and anomaly alerting

Who it is for: online shops, payment companies, marketplaces — anyone who loses money to fraud.

What it does: the same velocity and risk signals pointed at live traffic. Burst attacks, credential-stuffed takeovers, impossible-travel logins and bot swarms trip thresholds in real time; the alert email arrives with the sealed evidence already attached. The customer catches fraud while it is happening — and unlike a conventional fraud tool, holds court-grade proof of exactly what was caught, when, and what the system did about it.

3.5 Guardian — child safety under the Online Safety Act

Who it is for: consoles, games and social platforms with young users, now legally responsible for their safety.

What it does: a safety layer platforms embed in their own apps — their design, this engine underneath. Pattern-flagging of known grooming and manipulation approaches that never falsely tells a child a message is safe (verdicts are FLAGGED or UNRECOGNISED, never a false green light — a design ethic, not a feature); one-tap access to CEOP, Childline and 999 for every child; parental alerts on flags and panic events; and every safety event sealed to the chain — the duty-of-care evidence Ofcom requests. Message content is never stored, only a fingerprint. Families never pay; platforms pay the standard 50p per device. Endpoints: /api/guardian/pair, /flag, /checkin, /panic, /report.

3.6 The Sovereign Profile Notary — identity before impersonation

Who it is for: anyone with a public profile — which now means anyone. Generative models clone a face, a voice and a biography in minutes; executive impersonation and recruiter cloning are established attack economies.

What it does: at /notary, a profile — name, title, bio, links — is fingerprinted in the user's own browser and the fingerprint sealed with an official timestamp. The user receives a 12-character verification code for their bio on any platform: LinkedIn, X, a website footer, a CV. Any reader checks the code in seconds; an impersonation differing by one character fails publicly. What a clone can never produce is an earlier seal. Publication of display fields to the public registry is strictly opt-in; otherwise nothing but the fingerprint exists anywhere. Free, under a minute — and every unclaimed-code check invites the checker to seal their own profile: the product recruits its own users.

3.7 The Payment Notary — invoice fraud, structurally prevented

Who it is for: every business that sends invoices and every customer that pays them. Authorised push payment fraud costs the UK hundreds of millions of pounds a year, most commonly by intercepting a genuine invoice and switching the bank details.

What it does: at /pay-check, a business seals a fingerprint of its true payment details once — the full sort code and account number never leave the business's browser and are never stored; only the fingerprint plus a masked display survives, so the notary can never become the honeypot it exists to defeat. Every invoice then carries a 12-character code. Before paying, the customer enters the code and the details as printed on the invoice; the check passes only on an exact match with the sealed original. A switched sort code fails in ten seconds, before the money moves.

The decisive feature — the legal ratchet: every verification is itself sealed, and the payer receives a timestamped Verification Receipt: proof of exactly the "reasonable care" on which reimbursement decisions under the 2024 rules turn. The business holds proof of prevention; the payer holds proof of diligence; banks and insurers gain a standard they can request. Each party's legal exposure makes the others' participation rational — the regulation itself performs the adoption.

3.8 The public verification suite — the chain's open front door

Who it is for: everyone the other seven instruments make claims to — auditors, journalists, counterparties, sceptics.

What it does: /verify confirms whether any pasted text was sealed, and when — powering, among other things, the author's hash-chained public feed, in which every post is sealed and publicly checkable, the operator living under the discipline the product sells. /scan gives any company a free EU AI Act exposure scan — the top of the commercial funnel. /compliance-assistant answers regulation questions against the platform's own regulation map. Free, unauthenticated, and each one a door.

4. The open standard: ai.txt for the age of evidence

The web has a tradition of small text files that became infrastructure: robots.txt told crawlers the rules; security.txt told researchers where to report; ads.txt told buyers who really sells a site's inventory. Each began as one vendor's convention and became a norm because counterparties started expecting it.

sebbi.pro publishes the same move for AI compliance, at /ai-standard: a machine-readable declaration — the ai.txt / comply.txt convention — by which any organisation states what AI it runs, which governance layer seals its decisions, and where its evidence can be independently verified. The platform's own /api/regulation-map publishes the live mapping of engine capabilities to legal obligations, and every change to that map is itself sealed as a chain event — so even the compliance claims have an audit trail.

Standards are the purest form of capture: they cost nothing to adopt, they spread by counterparty expectation, and whoever defines the verification endpoint sits underneath every adopter. The declaration is free forever; the sealing it points to is the business.

5. The capture doctrine: how this wins

A systems view of this market says the prize is not a customer list — it is a standard position. The doctrine has three stages, each feeding the next.

5.1 Technical capture: win by architecture others cannot retrofit

Chains cannot be backfilled. A competitor who begins sealing today cannot manufacture last year; an incumbent who bolts a "blockchain module" onto a mutable stack still has the mutable stack, plus a checkpoint that itself must be trusted. Determinism, inline sealing and gapless receipts must be the first design decision, and here they were — four years ago. The consequence is a moat that deepens passively: time already sealed. Every day of unbroken history widens a gap that restarts at zero for anyone starting today. Technical capture is holding an asset competitors cannot buy at any price.

5.2 Network capture: every instrument recruits for every other

The eight instruments are one flywheel. Each sealed profile puts a code in a bio; each code drives checks; each failed check invites a seal. Each sealed business puts a code on invoices; each invoice trains payers to demand codes from other businesses. Each referral code pays its holder to recruit; each partner white-label deploys the engine under another brand's sales force. Every constituency captured makes the chain more valuable to every other — the classic network dynamic, but anchored to evidence rather than attention, which makes it durable: attention churns, history compounds.

5.3 Forced capture: when counterparties remove the buyer's choice

The endgame is the moment adoption stops being a purchasing decision. Banks assessing a reimbursement claim ask for the Verification Receipt. Insurers pricing cyber and E&O cover ask whether AI decisions are sealed. Acquirers' due-diligence checklists ask for gapless receipts. Regulators ask for the chain, because a chain is the only submission they do not have to take on faith. At that point the question a company faces is no longer "shall we buy this?" but "our counterparty requires this — where do we get it?" — and the answer is whoever holds the standard position built in stages one and two. Vendors sell; standards are demanded. The entire strategy of this platform is to be demanded.

7. Sovereign deployments: the enterprise edition of trustlessness

Banks, insurers, defence suppliers and public-sector bodies increasingly cannot ship decision data to third-party clouds at all. The platform's answer is the sovereign deployment: the same engine, air-gapped on the customer's own infrastructure, licensed through a cryptographic token system that validates entirely offline — no phone-home, no licence server, no external dependency, verified by mathematics rather than by connectivity, and hardened through a battery of stress tests. Automatic daily backups protect chain continuity; the customer owns the evidence outright. Sovereign deployments carry enterprise licensing and are the natural upsell for exactly the counterparties — banks, insurers — whose demands drive forced capture in Section 5.3. The instrument of adoption and the premium product are the same organisations.

11. Frequently asked questions

What evidence does the EU AI Act actually require?

Continuous risk management (Article 9), lifecycle record keeping (Article 12), transparent explanation (Article 13) and human oversight (Article 14). The engine emits all four per decision, inline, in under 30ms.

How is this different from ordinary logging?

Ordinary logs can be edited by whoever holds access. Here, editing any historic record breaks every seal after it, publicly. The operator cannot rewrite its own history — which is what makes the record evidence rather than testimony.

What is technical capture and forced capture?

Technical capture: winning through architecture others cannot retrofit — chains cannot be backfilled, so sealed history is an asset competitors cannot buy. Forced capture: the stage at which counterparties — banks, insurers, auditors, regulators — demand sealed evidence, removing the adoption decision from the buyer entirely. Section 5 sets out the full doctrine.

What is Brain?

Cryptographic governance for AI instructions: every version of a system prompt sealed with a timestamp, so an organisation can prove exactly what its AI was told to do, and when — the missing evidence layer for prompt-injection incidents and behaviour disputes.

How does the Payment Notary stop invoice fraud?

The business's true details are sealed once; every invoice carries a code; every payer checks in ten seconds. Switched details fail before the money moves — and the check itself is sealed, giving the payer a Verification Receipt evidencing reasonable care under the 2024 reimbursement rules.

Can I verify without an account?

Yes — every endpoint in Sections 3.8 and 4 is public.

What happens when my trial ends?

The engine pauses and presents a Stripe checkout reflecting your real device count. Your chain, keys and history remain intact; continue where you left off, or leave with everything still verifiable.
