The compliance gap.

The EU AI Act creates binding obligations for organisations deploying AI systems in high-risk categories — including credit scoring, employment tools, critical infrastructure, and law enforcement. These obligations include maintaining detailed logs of AI decision-making, ensuring human oversight, and demonstrating that systems operate within defined risk parameters.

Most organisations currently deploying AI have no infrastructure in place to produce this evidence. Consultancy documents and policy frameworks describe what should be done. They do not, by themselves, provide the technical mechanism to do it.

AILeash is built to fill that specific gap. It is not a dashboard, a report, or a framework — it is live infrastructure. An API endpoint sits between an AI system and its decisions, logging every action into a cryptographically chained audit record before the response is returned to the caller.

The enforcement timeline, accurately stated

Prohibited AI practices have been enforceable since February 2025, and GPAI model transparency obligations since August 2025 — both unaffected by recent developments. The high-risk system obligations most relevant to this platform were set to apply from 2 August 2026, but a provisional agreement (the "Digital Omnibus," 7 May 2026) proposes deferring that specific deadline to 2 December 2027, pending formal EU adoption. Organisations should treat compliance infrastructure as something to build ahead of enforcement either way — audit trails and documentation take time to establish properly, and cannot be retrofitted after an incident.

Technical architecture.

AILeash is built on pure Python with minimal external runtime dependencies. The data layer uses SQLite in WAL (Write-Ahead Logging) mode for concurrent read performance. The audit chain is maintained in a dedicated table with each entry containing a SHA-256 hash of the previous entry — forming an unbroken Merkle chain from genesis block to present.

// Section 04

The Merkle audit chain.

A Merkle chain provides a mathematically checkable guarantee of record integrity. Each entry contains a SHA-256 hash computed over the content of that entry plus the hash of the previous entry.

Any modification to any historical record — no matter how small — produces a different hash, which breaks every subsequent entry in the chain. The break is detectable by recomputing hashes forward from genesis. There is no way to alter a past record and repair the chain without redoing every hash after it, which is computationally the same work as building a new chain.

What this means in practice

A valid, unbroken chain demonstrates that the audit log has not been altered since the genesis block. This is a stronger integrity guarantee than a conventional append-only log, because tampering is mathematically detectable rather than merely against policy. Whether a given regulator or court accepts this as sufficient evidence in a specific case is a legal question outside the scope of this document — organisations should seek their own legal advice on evidentiary standards in their jurisdiction.

// Section 05

Sovereign deployment.

Cloud AI governance tools introduce a structural issue: the data required to demonstrate compliance leaves the customer's network and is stored by a third party. This raises data transfer questions under GDPR, introduces vendor dependency, and means the audit trail's trustworthiness partly rests on the vendor's own security posture.

AILeash is designed to run on the customer's own infrastructure. The engine, database, and audit chain operate within the customer's network perimeter. Decision data does not need to leave the customer's infrastructure to be logged, hashed, and chained.

GDPR alignment

Because data can remain within the customer's own network, cross-border data transfer questions and third-party processor obligations are reduced. This is a design choice that supports GDPR alignment — it is not itself a certification of compliance, which depends on the customer's full data handling practices.

Regulatory alignment.

AILeash is designed to support the audit and transparency obligations introduced by the EU AI Act for high-risk AI systems, as well as related frameworks. This section describes design intent, not a legal certification — organisations remain responsible for their own compliance assessment.

EU AI Act — Art. 12

Record-keeping obligations for high-risk AI systems. AILeash provides automatic, tamper-evident logging of AI-assisted decisions with precise timestamps.

EU AI Act — Art. 9

Risk management system requirements. AILeash's ALLOW / CHALLENGE / BLOCK framework provides a live risk control layer that can support this requirement.

GDPR — Art. 22

Automated individual decision-making. AILeash logs can help demonstrate human oversight and support explainability of AI-assisted decisions.
