The Sovereign Evidence Standard: tamper-evident governance for AI decisions, payments and identity

Justin Antony Dobson · Founder, Monop Content · sebbi.pro · July 2026 · Version 3.0 · Engine v6.5.0, live in production · Includes full product specifications and the seed investment case (Section 8)

Regulation has quietly changed what counts as an answer. The EU AI Act demands records of every consequential AI decision that survive hostile scrutiny. The UK's Online Safety Act demands provable duty of care. The 2024 mandatory reimbursement rules make banks and payers answerable for the care they took over every payment. In each case the required artefact is identical: evidence that cannot have been quietly rewritten — not by an attacker, not by an employee, not by the vendor itself. This paper describes a complete, live production system that generates such evidence as a by-product of normal operation at a marginal cost near zero; details each of its six products; sets out its economics in full; and presents the investment case for scaling its distribution.

1. The problem: every audit trail you can edit is a promise, not proof

Ask any compliance vendor how their audit trail works and the answer is a variation of "we log everything." The unasked question decides legal outcomes: who can edit those logs? The answer is always somebody — a database administrator, an engineer with production access, the vendor itself. A record that can be silently altered has no evidentiary weight the moment its integrity is questioned, and integrity is precisely what regulators, courts, insurers and due-diligence teams now question first.

Three legal regimes make this urgent simultaneously. The EU AI Act (Regulation 2024/1689) reaches general application in August 2026, with penalties up to 3% of global annual turnover or €15m per violation; its record-keeping duty (Article 12) presumes records a supervisory authority can rely on. (The proposed Digital Omnibus deferral moves the high-risk Annex III deadline to December 2027, pending formal adoption — a delay in enforcement, not in the obligation.) The UK Online Safety Act 2023 is in force now; Ofcom's investigations turn on whether a platform can evidence its safety decisions rather than merely describe them, and the ICO has already fined a major platform £12.7m under the Children's Code. The Payment Systems Regulator's mandatory reimbursement rules (October 2024) oblige banks to reimburse authorised push payment fraud up to £85,000 unless the payer failed to take reasonable care — placing a legal premium on provable diligence at both ends of every payment.

The market's response has been dashboards, policy documents and AI-watching-AI: artefacts that demonstrate effort, not truth. What follows is the alternative: evidence by construction.

2. The architecture

2.1 A deterministic engine below the model layer

The engine scores every event across nine weighted signals and returns one of three verdicts — ALLOW, CHALLENGE or BLOCK — with plain-language reasons. Because scoring is fixed mathematics rather than a learned model, it is deterministic: identical inputs produce identical outputs, forever. Any regulator or engineer can recompute any historic decision from the public specification at /api/spec. Probabilistic systems auditing probabilistic systems inherit the opacity they are meant to police; deterministic systems are the only ones that can honestly satisfy Article 13.

Thresholds: ALLOW below 0.35; CHALLENGE from 0.35; BLOCK from 0.70. A CHALLENGE verdict carries a hosted human-verification URL — the Article 14 oversight pathway — whose resolution is itself sealed.

2.2 The hash chain: tamper-evidence by construction

Every decision is sealed at the moment it is made: hashed with SHA-256 together with the seal of the record before it, so each block's integrity depends on the entire history beneath it. Alter one character of one historic record and every subsequent seal fails verification — publicly, at an open endpoint, no account required. The operator cannot rewrite its own records. A system that does not trust its own creator is the only kind whose records qualify as evidence.

seal(n) = SHA-256( seal(n−1) · timestamp · event · result )
verify: recompute from genesis; any mismatch localises tampering to a block index
latency: score + decide + seal + respond < 30ms inline; SonicBoom median 28ms
concurrency: tip read, hash and insert inside a single lock hold — no race window

2.3 Gapless receipts: proving what is absent

Tamper-evidence proves records were not altered; it does not prove records were not omitted. Every sealed decision therefore receives a per-key, strictly monotonic sequence number issued in the same transaction as the seal. A client holding receipts 47 and 49 can prove arithmetically that 48 exists and is missing from any disclosure. Completeness stops being a claim and becomes a calculation — a property the author has not observed in any competing product. Reconciliation is self-service at /api/coverage.

2.4 Zero dependencies as security posture and pricing power

The entire platform is pure Python with no external packages: no frameworks to patch, no supply chain to audit, no transitive compromise surface. It deploys as a single process and runs full production for approximately £10 per month. That figure is the pricing mechanism, not trivia. Incumbents charge £50,000 a year partly because their cost base demands it; a platform whose infrastructure costs less than a streaming subscription can charge 50p per device and remain profitable — and can give away 90 days at negligible marginal cost. Cost base is pricing power; architecture is the moat.

3. The six products in full

3.1 AILeash — AI decision governance

Who it is for: any organisation whose AI makes decisions about people — banks approving loans, insurers pricing policies, fintechs blocking payments, marketplaces banning accounts, HR platforms screening candidates.

What it does: one POST /api/govern call per decision returns the verdict, plain-English reasons, the seal, the chain block index and the gapless receipt sequence — the complete Article 9/12/13/14 evidence set, generated inline in under 30ms. BLOCK verdicts trigger a real-time alert email with the sealed evidence attached, throttled to one per hour per key so an attack cannot also flood the inbox. Live monitoring at /api/pulse; billing transparency at /api/usage.

Integration: any system that can send an HTTPS POST with JSON — every modern backend, no-code tools, mobile via the customer's own server. No SDK, no library, nothing added to the stack. A step-by-step installation guide with the customer's own key baked into the code samples is emailed automatically on signup.

3.2 SonicBoom — the compliance layer for existing AI stacks

Who it is for: companies already running AI on AWS, Azure, Google Cloud, OpenAI or Anthropic that need compliance without touching latency budgets.

What it does: one line of code alongside the existing AI call adds a sealed compliance record to every request — before the call to gate it, or after to seal it — at a median 28ms. Governance that never becomes the latency problem it exists to prevent. Powered by the same Sebdog engine core as AILeash.

3.3 Sentinel — fraud and anomaly alerting

Who it is for: online shops, payment companies, marketplaces — anyone who loses money to fraud.

What it does: the same velocity and risk signals pointed at live traffic. Burst attacks, credential-stuffed takeovers, impossible-travel logins and bot swarms trip thresholds in real time; the alert email arrives with the sealed evidence already attached. The customer catches fraud while it is happening, holding proof of exactly what was caught and when.

3.4 Guardian — child safety under the Online Safety Act

Who it is for: consoles, games and social platforms with young users, now legally responsible for their safety.

What it does: a safety layer platforms embed in their own apps — their design, this engine underneath. Pattern-flagging of known grooming and manipulation approaches that never falsely tells a child a message is safe (verdicts are FLAGGED or UNRECOGNISED, never a false green light); one-tap access to CEOP, Childline and 999 for every child; parental alerts on flags and panic events; and every safety event sealed to the chain — the duty-of-care evidence Ofcom requests. Message content is never stored, only a fingerprint. Families never pay; platforms pay the standard 50p per device. Endpoints: /api/guardian/pair, /flag, /checkin, /panic, /report.

3.5 The Sovereign Profile Notary — identity before impersonation

Who it is for: anyone with a public profile — which now means anyone. Generative models clone a face, a voice and a biography in minutes; executive impersonation and recruiter cloning are established attack economies.

What it does: at /notary, a profile — name, title, bio, links — is fingerprinted in the user's own browser and the fingerprint sealed with an official timestamp. The user receives a 12-character verification code for their bio on any platform: LinkedIn, X, a website footer, a CV. Any reader checks the code in seconds; an impersonation differing by one character fails publicly. What a clone can never produce is an earlier seal. Publication of display fields to the public registry is strictly opt-in; otherwise nothing but the fingerprint exists anywhere. Free, under a minute — and every unclaimed-code check invites the checker to seal their own profile: the product recruits its own users.

3.6 The Payment Notary — invoice fraud, structurally prevented

Who it is for: every business that sends invoices and every customer that pays them. Authorised push payment fraud costs the UK hundreds of millions of pounds a year, most commonly by intercepting a genuine invoice and switching the bank details.

What it does: at /pay-check, a business seals a fingerprint of its true payment details once — the full sort code and account number never leave the business's browser and are never stored; only the fingerprint plus a masked display survives. Every invoice then carries a 12-character code. Before paying, the customer enters the code and the details as printed on the invoice; the check passes only on an exact match with the sealed original. A switched sort code fails in ten seconds, before the money moves.

The decisive feature — the legal ratchet: every verification is itself sealed, and the payer receives a timestamped Verification Receipt: proof of exactly the "reasonable care" on which reimbursement decisions under the 2024 rules turn. The business holds proof of prevention; the payer holds proof of diligence; banks and insurers gain a standard they can request. Each party's legal exposure makes the others' participation rational — the regulation itself performs the adoption.

5. Why this is years ahead, not incrementally better

Retrofitting tamper-evidence onto a trust-based platform is installing a foundation beneath a finished building: every existing table, workflow and integration assumes records are mutable. The incumbent path — bolt a "blockchain module" onto a conventional stack — leaves the editable stores in place and adds a checkpoint that itself must be trusted. Evidence by construction cannot be added later; it must be the first decision. Here, it was — four years ago.

There is also an asset no competitor can purchase: time already sealed. A chain's evidentiary value compounds with unbroken history, and history cannot be backfilled — a chain started today cannot manufacture last year. The strategic consequence, which this paper terms the shift from technical capture to forced capture, is that counterparties drive adoption rather than vendors: insurers, banks, acquirers and regulators asking "show me the sealed record" remove the buying decision from the buyer.

6. Business model in full

Core pricing. Everything free for 90 days: full engine, unlimited devices, no card, installation guide on signup. Thereafter 50p per unique device per month, metered on the real devices observed using each key — never a self-reported number. When a trial ends the engine pauses and presents a Stripe checkout reflecting the actual device count; the customer's chain, keys and history remain intact whether they pay or leave. No tiers, no seats, no sales calls, no lock-in — a lapsed customer's records stay verifiable forever.

Partner margin. Resellers and platforms white-label at any price above the 50p base and keep the entire margin: a partner charging £1.99 per device keeps £1.49, forever, on infrastructure they never operate.

Referrals. Every signup receives a permanent referral code paying 10p per referred device per month with no cap and no expiry — distribution that compounds without spend.

Free instruments as the funnel. The Profile Notary and Payment Notary are free and self-propagating: every bio code and every invoice code advertises the checker; every failed check invites the checker to seal their own. Each captured user stands one step from the commercial platform.

Unit economics. Platform infrastructure: ~£10/month. Marginal cost per additional customer: effectively zero. A single 20-device customer covers the entire cost base; everything beyond is gross margin. Four years of product were built for under £1,000 of total infrastructure spend.

9. Frequently asked questions

What evidence does the EU AI Act actually require?

Continuous risk management (Article 9), lifecycle record keeping (Article 12), transparent explanation (Article 13) and human oversight (Article 14). The engine emits all four per decision, inline, in under 30ms.

How is this different from ordinary logging?

Ordinary logs can be edited by whoever holds access. Here, editing any historic record breaks every seal after it, publicly. The operator cannot rewrite its own history — which is what makes the record evidence rather than testimony.

How does the Payment Notary stop invoice fraud?

The business's true details are sealed once; every invoice carries a code; every payer checks in ten seconds. Switched details fail the check before the money moves — and the check itself is sealed, giving the payer a Verification Receipt evidencing reasonable care under the 2024 reimbursement rules.

Can I verify without an account?

Yes — every endpoint in Section 4 is public.

What happens when my trial ends?

The engine pauses and presents a Stripe checkout reflecting your real device count. Your chain, keys and history remain intact; continue where you left off, or leave with everything still verifiable.
